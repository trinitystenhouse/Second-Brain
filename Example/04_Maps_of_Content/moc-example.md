## What this hub is for

A worked example spoke, kept deliberately small. The subject is how knowledge graphs actually work, so reading it teaches you the method and demonstrates the format at the same time — this file is what every spoke's hub should look like.

**Delete the whole `Example/` folder once you have a spoke of your own.** Nothing depends on it.

## What to notice about the format

- Every source note has a real, working URL. Check one. That standard is absolute — a fabricated citation looks identical to a real one right up until the day it matters.
- Every link states why it exists. Scan the Connections sections: none of them is a bare `[[link]]`.
- The permanent notes have a "What would change my mind" section. It is the section that stops a note quietly turning into an unexamined assumption two years from now.
- Nothing here is padded. Three sources and two notes is a legitimate size for a spoke that has done its job.

## Open questions

These are the most valuable lines in any hub — what the next research pass picks up, and what stops a spoke becoming a museum. Real ones, not placeholders:

- Where does the linking discipline stop paying for itself? There is presumably a vault size below which the overhead is not worth it, and the honest answer is probably "a few hundred notes", which would mean the first month feels like pure cost.
- Does an AI reading the graph change what good structure looks like? The conventions here predate assistants that can read the whole vault at once. Some of them might be load-bearing for a human and irrelevant to a machine, or the reverse.
- Is "one idea per note" doing real work, or is it a proxy for "know what you are claiming"? [[zettelkasten-de-atomicity-guide]] refuses to give a word count, which suggests the authors suspect the same thing.
