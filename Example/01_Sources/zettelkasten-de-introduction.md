## Summary

The standard modern explanation of the Zettelkasten method, the slip-box system associated with the sociologist Niklas Luhmann. Its central claim is that a Zettelkasten is a tool for thinking and writing rather than a place to store things you have read, and that the difference comes down to structure. Each note carries three components: an identifier that gives it a fixed address so other notes can point at it, a body containing a single thought written in your own words rather than copied, and a set of references to its sources and to related notes.

The argument that does most of the work is about linking. The system's value is not in the notes but in the connections between them — and, crucially, a link is only worth something if it states *why* it exists. A bare pointer from one note to another records that you once noticed a relationship; a link with its reasoning attached records the relationship itself. Ordinary note-taking, by contrast, is described as producing a bloated mess: a growing pile with no navigability, where the cost of finding something again rises with the size of the collection.

## Key takeaways

- A note gets a stable identifier so it can be addressed and linked. Time-based (`202006110955`), sequential (`1a`) and title-based schemes all work; this vault uses title-based, which is what `[[wikilinks]]` are.
- Write the body in your own words. Copying defers the understanding rather than doing it.
- Always state the reason for a link. Without it the connection is shallow and does not generate anything later.
- One discrete idea per note — the principle of atomicity, covered properly in the companion guide.
- The distinguishing feature against ordinary notes is hypertextual, non-linear navigation: you arrive somewhere by following a chain of reasoning rather than by remembering a folder name.

## Relevance to you

This is the source of the two conventions the vault enforces everywhere: every note links to at least two others, and every link says why. Those rules look like bureaucracy for the first fortnight and then become the reason the vault is worth anything — they are what makes the collection more valuable than the sum of its notes, and they are also what makes it possible for an AI to reason over it.
