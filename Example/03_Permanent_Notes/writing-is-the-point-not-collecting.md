## The idea in one sentence

Saving a source is not learning from it, and a vault full of unread saved things is a more elaborate version of an open browser tab.

## Why I think this

The intuition that collecting feels productive is exactly backwards, and there is decent evidence for why. [[wikipedia-testing-effect]] covers the finding that retrieving information from memory beats passively restudying it, with the gap widening as time passes — and the refinement that matters here is that harder retrievals produce more benefit than easy ones.

Map that onto note-taking and the conclusion is uncomfortable. Highlighting is recognition: the text is in front of you, you are agreeing with it. Copying a passage is transcription, which requires no retrieval at all. Closing the tab and writing what the argument actually was, in your own words, is retrieval — which is why it is annoying, and why it is the only one of the three that does anything. This is the same reason [[zettelkasten-de-introduction]] insists the body of a note be written in your own words: copying defers the understanding rather than performing it.

The practical failure mode is well known to anyone who has abandoned a note system. You accumulate for six months, the collection becomes too large to review, and the sunk cost of all that saving makes it worse rather than better — you now have a large debt of unprocessed material and no idea which of it was any good. A vault that grows faster than you can write into it is not an asset.

## What it changes

- **The source note's "Relevance to you" section is the actual work.** Summary and takeaways are mostly transcription; the relevance paragraph cannot be written without having understood the thing. If it cannot be filled in, that is a signal the source should probably not be filed.
- **Prefer fewer sources, properly processed.** Four real notes beat ten thin ones — which is why `kg-research` is told not to pad.
- **An unprocessed inbox is a warning sign, not a to-do list.** If `_staged/` keeps growing, the intake rate is wrong and the answer is to ingest less rather than to find more time.
- **The open questions in a map of content are retrieval prompts.** Revisiting them is spaced practice on your own material, which is why the convention exists and why an empty open-questions section means the spoke has stopped being alive.

## What would change my mind

If someone ran a vault purely as a searchable archive — no synthesis, everything copy-pasted — and still got real value from it over years, the claim would be too strong. That is plausible for reference material where you genuinely only need to find the passage again, like API documentation or case law. The argument here is about material you want to *think with*, and I should be more careful to say so rather than treating all notes as the same kind of thing.
